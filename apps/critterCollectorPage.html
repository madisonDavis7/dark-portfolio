Critter Collector

Background

This app design was created as a pair project for a UI Design class I took in college. When I was brainstorming ideas for this I knew I wanted it to have to do with cats, have a cute aesthetic, and be for all ages. On my commute to work or class I would get very excited and take a photo of any cats I saw, so my original idea was a cat collecting app. This branched into a critter collecting app after the first round of class feedback.

Tools and Technology

Figma Qualtrics Google Suite

Key Features

In-app Camera

Users can efficiently snap a photo of a critter with the app's built-in camera that allows for endless retakes.

Socials

A secure social functions lets you add friends, quickly share your critters, and leave comments on friends' posts!

Customizability

Upload a profile picture, create collections, change the theme and more

Working Together

Because this was a pair project we had to decide how we were going to combine our ideas seamlessly, break up the workload, and communicate clearly what we were working on. This was a semester long project. I ended up taking the lead on the design aspect because I had a clearer idea of what the app would look like, had lots of experience with Figma, and have a love for designing in general. My partner took on more of the work with getting our test plan for user testing created and ensured we would be prepared.

Challenges

There were no huge challenges during this project. There were a few technical issues during two of our testing seesions but they were quickly resolved and the sessions finished smoothly. It was a bit difficult getting contradictive feedback from our evaluations and deciding what changes should be made, in the end I chose to go with the changes I felt I agreed with because in the end it was my original design and there were no real clients involved.

User Testing

Who

Six participants recruited from friends, classmates, and roommates representing a range of ages and comfort with mobile apps.

Why

To surface any usability issues in the prototype and validate our design decisions before the final class presentation.

What

In-person sessions using a detailed test plan with task-based scenarios, observation checklists, and follow-up interview questions.

Results

Across all six sessions, participants found the core critter-capturing flow intuitive and enjoyable. The most consistent feedback pointed to a few unclear navigation labels and a desire for more visual feedback when saving a critter. We also heard that the social sharing flow felt a step too long. After analyzing the results, I made targeted updates to the nav labels, added a confirmation animation on save, and trimmed the share flow down by one screen.

Analysis

This project deepened my understanding of 3D math, coordinate systems, and WebGL-based rendering through Three.js. I also learned how to work with public scientific APIs and parse large datasets efficiently in the browser. Beyond the technical side, designing for a dark, data-heavy interface pushed me to think more carefully about contrast, hierarchy, and how to guide a user's eye without cluttering the screen.
